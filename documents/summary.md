# Summary of MIMIC Resources and Readmission Research

## MIMIC-IV Ecosystem Overview
MIMIC-IV (v3.1, Oct 2024) provides de-identified ICU and hospital EHR records for more than 364K patients at BIDMC, split across the `hosp` module (546K admissions) and the `icu` module (94K stays). Privacy safeguards replace PHI, shift timestamps into the 2100–2200 range, and bundle anchor metadata (`anchor_year`, `anchor_year_group`, `anchor_age`) so longitudinal analyses stay internally consistent while cross-patient calendars remain invalid. Access requires PhysioNet credentials, an active DUA, and CITI training; once approved, BigQuery projects `mimiciv_v3_1_hosp` and `mimiciv_v3_1_icu` supply the managed copies, with legacy datasets now pointing to v3.1. Companion releases broaden coverage: MIMIC-IV-Note (331K discharge summaries, 2.3M radiology reports), MIMIC-IV-ED (≈425K emergency stays), MIMIC-CXR (377K chest x-rays), and the Llama-3.1-derived MIMIC-IV-Ext-22MCTS event timeline set. When publishing derivatives, append “Ext” to dataset names, register parent projects on PhysioNet, and cite the MIMIC-IV DOI, the 2023 Scientific Data paper, and the PhysioNet standard.

## Version and Usage Highlights
Recent releases fix lab catalog inconsistencies (v3.1), add 2020–2022 encounters and out-of-hospital mortality (v3.0), and introduce provider identifiers while removing held-out subjects (v2.2–v2.1). Researchers should expect raw clinical irregularities, validate joins across `subject_id`/`hadm_id`, and respect the one-year mortality censoring window. For BigQuery work, prefer the v3.1 tables, extract reusable logic into modules, and document analytic notebooks with clear data provenance.

## 2024–2025 Readmission Modeling on MIMIC-IV
Recent NLP-driven readmission studies blend structured EHR signals with transformer-based representations of notes, pushing AUROC into the 0.65–0.87 range. The CRL-MMNAR framework (arXiv 2025) leads with causal fusion over 20K patients (AUROC 0.87) and open-source code. EMERGE (CIKM 2024) pairs retrieval-augmented generation with Clinical-LongFormer to weather sparse modalities (AUROC 0.81). ETHOS (npj Digital Medicine 2024) demonstrates zero-shot trajectory transformers on the full cohort (AUROC 0.75), while ClinicalT5 (PLOS ONE 2025) and a heart-failure-focused Word2Vec/BERT pipeline round out the landscape with percentile gains over traditional baselines despite lower absolute scores. Code artifacts are available for four of the five leading papers, supporting reproducibility and ablation benchmarking.

## Practical Research Takeaways
Multimodal fusion and causal reasoning are now table stakes for competitive readmission prediction, but performance remains sensitive to cohort selection and missing-note handling. Investigators should log preprocessing steps, evaluate against established scores like LACE, and guard against data leakage when deriving features from de-identified timelines. Always treat downstream datasets as sensitive, store access credentials outside version control, and link analyses back to the appropriate PhysioNet projects.
